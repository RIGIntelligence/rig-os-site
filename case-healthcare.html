The Situation

A regional health system operating multiple emergency departments was facing a crisis. Average wait times had climbed to 3 hours. Left-without-being-seen (LWBS) rates exceeded 12%. Staff burnout was accelerating turnover. Patients were diverting to competing hospitals.

The system had invested in multiple point solutions — a patient tracking board, a triage optimization tool, a bed management system. None of them talked to each other. The charge nurse was the human integration layer, manually coordinating between systems and making bed assignment decisions based on experience and a whiteboard.

The Diagnosis

We conducted an AI Opportunity Report focused on the ED patient flow. The audit revealed:

  • Bed assignment decisions took 15-30 minutes because they required checking 3 separate systems
  • Discharge processes stalled because housekeeping was not notified in real-time when patients were ready to move
  • Triage was accurate but slow — bottlenecked by documentation requirements, not clinical assessment
  • No predictive model for incoming volume — staffing was based on historical averages, not real-time demand
  • 12% LWBS rate represented approximately $8M in lost revenue annually

What We Built

An IntOps patient flow operating model with four integrated workflows:

  • Real-time demand prediction — ML model trained on 3 years of visit data, weather, events, and seasonal patterns. Predicts ED volume 4-6 hours ahead with 89% accuracy. Staffing adjusts dynamically.
  • Automated bed assignment — AI recommends optimal bed assignments based on patient acuity, anticipated care needs, bed availability, and cleaning status. Charge nurse approves with one click — human-in-the-loop on every assignment.
  • Discharge acceleration — Real-time notification chain: physician marks ready → housekeeping notified → transport notified → bed available. Reduced discharge-to-bed-ready time from 90 minutes to 20 minutes.
  • Triage documentation assistant — AI-assisted documentation that captures triage assessment in natural language and maps to structured fields. Reduced triage documentation time by 40% without changing clinical workflow.

Governance

Every clinical AI system was deployed with:

  • Human-in-the-loop oversight on all patient care decisions
  • Full audit trail of AI recommendations and human actions
  • Bias monitoring for patient demographics
  • Weekly quality reviews and monthly clinical committee oversight
  • SB 24-205 compliant governance documentation

Results

Within 6 months of full deployment:

  • Average ED wait time reduced from 3 hours to 15 minutes
  • LWBS rate dropped from 12% to under 3%
  • Patient satisfaction scores increased 34 points
  • Staff overtime reduced by 22%
  • Estimated $6M+ in recovered revenue from reduced LWBS
