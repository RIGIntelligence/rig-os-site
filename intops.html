IntOps: Intelligence Operations

IntOps is the operating layer between your AI tools and your business accountability. It is the reason AI becomes a permanent business function instead of a pilot that dies when the champion leaves.

Why AI Projects Fail

$4.6 million. That is the average cost of an AI initiative that never reaches production, according to Gartner. And the failure rate is not improving — it is getting worse as organizations add more tools without adding operating infrastructure.

The pattern is consistent across every industry and every company size:

  • ✕ Executive gets excited about AI capability
  • ✕ Vendor runs a proof of concept
  • ✕ Demo looks great in the conference room
  • ✕ No one owns the workflow in production
  • ✕ No measurement framework exists
  • ✕ Champion moves to a new role
  • ✕ Tool sits unused in a Slack channel

The technology is not the problem. The absence of an operating model is the problem. That is what IntOps solves.

"We had 14 AI tools across 6 departments. Nobody could tell me which ones were working, which ones we were paying for, and which ones were making decisions about patients."

— Chief Operating Officer, Regional Health System

The IntOps Thesis

AI should be managed with the same rigor as finance, HR, or IT. It needs an owner, a budget, a measurement framework, governance policies, and a capability development plan. IntOps is that management layer.

The Five Layers

IntOps is built in five layers, each depending on the one below it. Skip a layer and the system collapses. Build them in order and AI becomes as reliable as any other business function.

Layer 01 — Intelligence Foundation

See Everything

Before you can govern AI, you have to know where it is. Layer 1 inventories every AI tool, model, vendor, and data source across your organization. We map decision flows — where does AI output influence a human decision? Where does it make decisions autonomously? Where is risk concentrated?

Deliverables: AI inventory, decision flow map, risk heat map, data lineage documentation, vendor assessment.

Layer 02 — Workflow Engine

Automate What Matters

We do not automate everything. We automate the workflows that already have a business owner, a measurable output, and a clear ROI. One workflow in production is worth more than six AI demos in a slide deck.

Each workflow gets: an owner, a measurement framework, integration with existing systems, human-in-the-loop gates where required, and an escalation path when the AI is uncertain.

Deliverables: Workflow specifications, system integrations, testing protocols, production deployment, monitoring dashboards.

Layer 03 — Governance & Compliance

Make It Accountable

Compliance is not a legal memo pinned to a SharePoint site. It is an operating system that runs inside the workflow, not beside it. Layer 3 builds the governance infrastructure: risk classification, audit trails, human oversight requirements, incident response protocols, and regulatory mapping.

For Colorado businesses: this layer maps directly to SB 24-205 requirements — AI inventory, risk classification, impact assessments, and consumer notification protocols.

Deliverables: Risk classification framework, audit trail system, governance policies, compliance roadmap, incident response playbook.

Layer 04 — Performance Intelligence

Measure What Moves

Every AI system gets measured by the business metric it was built to move. Not "model accuracy" or "user satisfaction" — the actual business outcome. Wait times. Revenue recovered. FTEs redeployed. Cycle time reduced. Cost per decision.

Layer 4 builds the measurement infrastructure: dashboards, alerting, trend analysis, and quarterly business reviews tied to AI performance.

Deliverables: KPI framework, executive dashboards, alerting system, quarterly review templates, ROI tracking.

Layer 05 — Organizational Capability

Own It Forever

IntOps is not a retainer dependency. It is a capability transfer. Layer 5 trains your team to operate, extend, and evolve the AI operating model after RIG's engagement ends.

We design a structured roll-off: full engagement → reduced scope → advisory only → complete handoff. The goal is always to make ourselves unnecessary.

Deliverables: Team training program, operating playbook, role definitions, escalation protocols, evolution roadmap.
